          Step 3 of 3 · Deliver It
        

        

        
Delivery coaching

        
Your story is written. Now make sure you deliver it like you mean it. These reminders are the difference between "that was fine" and "I want to work with this person."


        

          Energy
          
Walk in with a full battery and give some of it away. If you dial down to match a low-energy interviewer, you both leave flat. Bring the energy first. You're not being "too much" -- you're being memorable.

        


        

          Tone
          
Speak like you already work there. Confident, not auditioning. You're having a conversation with a future teammate, not reciting a monologue to a judge. Drop the "Thank you for this opportunity" energy.

        


        

          Pacing
          
The biggest mistake is rushing. Slow down at the pivot points. When you say "and that's when I realized..." or "which led me to..." -- pause for half a beat. Let the through-line land.

        


        

          The Ending
          
Your last sentence should be the most specific one. It should name THIS company or THIS role. The interviewer should feel like you wrote this story for them, not for 20 other companies.

        


        

          Practice Method
          
Record yourself on your phone. Listen back. You'll catch every filler word, every rushed section, every spot where the energy dips. Do this 3 times and you'll walk in knowing this story cold.

Resume Checklist

      
The stuff that actually matters when someone spends 15 seconds scanning your resume. Check off items as you go.

      
0 / 20 completed


      

        Format & Structure
        
One page (two if you have 5+ years of experience)

        
Consistent formatting throughout: same font sizes, bullet styles, date alignment

        
Clear section headers: Summary, Experience, Education, Skills, Projects

        
Readable font (11-12pt body), enough white space, no walls of text

      


      

        Content Quality
        
Summary is 2-3 sentences max, specific to the role you're targeting

        
Every bullet starts with a strong action verb (built, analyzed, shipped, led, designed)

        
Bullets follow "did X by doing Y, resulting in Z" structure where possible

        
Numbers and metrics in at least half your bullets (%, $, users, time saved)

        
No generic filler ("team player", "self-starter", "passionate about technology")

        
Each role has 2-4 bullets, not 6+

      


      

        Tailoring
        
Skills section matches the job description keywords (PM: roadmaps, PRDs, A/B testing; Analytics: SQL, dashboards, KPIs)

        
Most relevant experience is listed first, not necessarily most recent

        
Projects section highlights work relevant to the target role

        
Title/summary repositioned for each application (PM resume vs. analyst resume vs. research resume)

      


      

        Common Mistakes
        
No typos or grammatical errors (seriously, read it out loud)

        
No orphan lines or awkward page breaks

        
All links work (LinkedIn, GitHub, portfolio)

        
PDF filename is professional (FirstLast_Resume.pdf, not "resume final v3 copy.pdf")

        
Contact info is current and complete (email, LinkedIn, phone, location)

        
No references section or "references available upon request"

      

    

  


  
  

    

      
Behavioral Question Bank

      
Common questions organized by theme. Click any question to see tips on how to approach it.


      

        
Leadership & Initiative

        
Tell me about a time you took ownership of something outside your role.▾
Focus on WHY you stepped up (gap you noticed, problem nobody was solving). Show initiative without badmouthing the team. End with what changed because you acted.

        
Describe a time you had to lead without formal authority.▾
Great for PM roles. Show how you influenced through data, relationships, or framing rather than title. Emphasize the outcome and how others responded.

        
Tell me about a project you initiated on your own.▾
Show you can identify problems worth solving. Walk through how you built the case, got buy-in, and executed. Interviewers want to see you can go from zero to one.

      


      

        
Conflict & Disagreement

        
Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate or manager.▾
Never make the other person the villain. Show you listened first, then made your case with evidence. If you were wrong, say so. If you were right, show how you handled it with respect.

        
How do you handle pushback on your ideas?▾
Good answer = you seek to understand the pushback before defending. Show that you can separate ego from the idea. Bonus: mention a time pushback made your idea better.

        
Describe a situation where you had to work with a difficult stakeholder.▾
Frame "difficult" as misaligned incentives, not personal problems. Show empathy for their position, then explain how you found common ground or a workable compromise.

      


      

        
Failure & Learning

        
Tell me about a time you failed.▾
Pick a real failure, not a humble brag. Show self-awareness about what went wrong and be specific about what you changed afterward. The learning matters more than the failure.

        
Describe a project that didn't go as planned.▾
Focus on how you adapted. Did you re-scope, re-prioritize, communicate early about delays? Show you can handle ambiguity and pivot without losing the thread.

        
What's a mistake you made that taught you something important?▾
Be genuine. The best answers show a shift in how you think or work, not just "I learned to double-check my work." Systemic changes > surface fixes.

      


      

        
Data & Metrics

        
Tell me about a time you used data to influence a decision.▾
Walk through your process: what question you were answering, what data you pulled, how you analyzed it, and how you presented it. The "influence" part is key, not just the analysis.

        
How do you decide what to measure?▾
Show you think about leading vs. lagging indicators, that you tie metrics to user outcomes, and that you're aware of vanity metrics. Use a real example.

        
Describe a time the data surprised you.▾
Great opportunity to show intellectual honesty. Did you dig deeper or accept the surface result? Show curiosity and rigor. The best answers end with a non-obvious insight.

      


      

        
Collaboration & Teamwork

        
Tell me about a time you worked across functions.▾
Highlight the challenge of aligning different perspectives (eng wants feasibility, design wants polish, business wants speed). Show how you bridged those gaps.

        
How do you get buy-in from engineers or designers?▾
Answer: by understanding what they care about. Engineers want clear specs and context for why. Designers want user problems, not solutions. Show you adapt your pitch.

        
Describe your approach to giving feedback.▾
Be specific and direct but kind. Show that you give feedback regularly (not just in reviews), that you focus on behavior not character, and that you ask for feedback too.

      

    

  


  
  

    

      
Product Sense Practice

      
Practice prompts for the three main product interview categories. Each one includes a framework to get you started.


      
Product Design

      

        Design
        
Design a feature for Spotify that helps users discover music through friends.

        
Framework: Start with user segments (who benefits most?). Define the core problem (discovery is overwhelming). Brainstorm 3 solutions, pick one using prioritization criteria (impact, effort, alignment). Walk through the user flow. Define success metrics.

      

      

        Design
        
How would you improve the checkout experience on Amazon for first-time buyers?

        
Framework: Clarify scope (mobile? desktop? specific market?). Map the current flow and identify pain points. Prioritize by drop-off data if available. Propose 2-3 changes with rationale. Define metrics: conversion rate, time to complete, support tickets.

      

      

        Design
        
Design a product that helps college students find study groups.

        
Framework: Define the user persona (freshman vs. senior, STEM vs. humanities). Identify the core problem through user needs. Consider existing solutions (Discord, GroupMe). Propose a differentiated approach. Walk through key screens. Think about cold start and network effects.

      


      
Estimation

      

        Estimation
        
How many Uber rides happen in San Francisco on a Friday night?

        
Framework: Start with SF population (~870K). Estimate % going out on Friday (~15%). Of those, % using rideshare (~40%). Average rides per person (1.5 round trip). Sense-check: does ~78K rides feel right? Compare to known data points.

      

      

        Estimation
        
Estimate the annual revenue of a single Starbucks location.

        
Framework: Hours open (~14/day). Estimate customers per hour by time (morning rush: 80, midday: 30, afternoon: 40, evening: 20). Average transaction ($5.50). Multiply out daily, then yearly. Account for weekday vs. weekend differences.

      


      
Strategy & Metrics

      

        Strategy
        
Instagram Reels engagement is down 10% month over month. What do you do?

        
Framework: Don't jump to solutions. First clarify: is this across all users or a segment? Is it seasonal? Did a competitor launch something? Segment by geography, new vs. returning, content type. Form hypotheses, prioritize investigation, then propose experiments.

      

      

        Strategy
        
You're the PM for Google Maps. How do you decide what to build next quarter?

        
Framework: Start with the team's north star metric. Audit current performance against goals. Gather inputs: user research, support tickets, competitive landscape, eng capacity. Use a prioritization framework (RICE or ICE). Present a theme-based roadmap, not a feature list.

      

      

        Metrics
        
How would you measure the success of LinkedIn's "People You May Know" feature?

        
Framework: Define the goal (growing user networks). Primary metric: connection request rate from PYMK. Secondary: acceptance rate, profile views from PYMK, weekly connections added. Guardrails: spam reports, block rate, irrelevant suggestion feedback. Consider leading vs. lagging indicators.
